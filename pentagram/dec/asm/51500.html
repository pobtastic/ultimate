51500: Build Room Scenery
Used by the routine at BuildRoom.
Makes the scenery objects of a room, from the data of the room, which starts at Room_00 and has a record for each of the rooms. A record is the room number, then a length byte (which counts itself and the bytes after it), then a byte holding the ink colour of the room in bits 0-2 and the size of the room in bits 3-7, then the features of the room and then the groups of templates. The size is the index of one of the three 3-byte entries at RoomSizeTable, which hold the distance from the centre of the room to the walls on the x axis and the y axis, and the height of the floor.
The features are pairs of bytes: the number of a feature and a parameter, ended by 255 (or by the end of the data of the room, as 43 of the rooms have no 255). The number is the index into the table of addresses at FeatureTable. A feature is a list of the eight bytes of one or more objects (the type, x, y, z, the sizes along each axis and the flags) that ends with a zero in place of the type of another object. These are the doors and their frames, and the objects that are fixed to the walls. The parameter is stored in the room field of each of the objects in the feature, which makes it the number of the room that a door leads to.
Each group of templates that follows is a byte holding the number of a template in bits 3-7 and, in bits 0-2, one less than the number of cells that follow, and then a byte for each of the cells. A cell holds the x position in bits 0-2 and the y position in bits 3-5 (in steps of 16 from 72), and the height in bits 6-7 (in steps of 12 above the floor). A template is a list of the five bytes of one or more objects (the type, the sizes along each axis and the flags) that ends with a zero in place of the type of another object, and an object is made from it for each of the cells. The template numbers are the indexes into the table of addresses that starts at TemplateTable, which a group with the number 31 moves on by 32 templates, though no room has one. The last group of rooms 13 and 108 is cut short by the end of their data.
The objects are made from the last of the object slots, which is at ObjectsTable_Last, and downwards. The first feature of a room is always a door, with its frame in the last slot and the door in the one before, so that the doors are found by FindDoor in the slots at the top. The player and the creatures have the slots at the start.
